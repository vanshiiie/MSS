Regulation of phase-separating structures in soft materials

My research interests lie in Statistical Mechanics and Soft Matter Physics, with a focus on nonequilibrium phenomena and phase separation kinetics in complex systems.

I use analytical and computational approaches, including coarse-grained models, Molecular Dynamics (MD), and Dissipative Particle Dynamics (DPD) simulations in LAMMPS, to study phase separation, coarsening, and self-assembly in binary, multicomponent, and polymeric systems.

My work aims to understand the interplay between thermodynamics, kinetics, and external control parameters in governing morphology evolution and stability.

Somewhere between polymers, simulations, and too many plots, I try to understand how soft materials organize themselves. I’m interested in phase separation, self-assembly, and the dynamics that turn simple molecular interactions into complex structures.

Most of my work happens on a computer, where I let polymers do the experimenting and I try to figure out why they behave the way they do.

Outside the lab, I like exploring new places, travelling, and skating.
