Research

The research interests of our group lie broadly in the field of Soft Matter and Statistical Physics.

We study the dynamic and structural properties of phase ordering in unstable, far-from-equilibrium systems. We explore how their behaviour is measured and characterised, through phenomena such as nucleation, spinodal decomposition, late-stage growth, and coarsening. We use a combination of analytical and computational tools — coarse-grained field-theoretical models, Monte Carlo (MC), Molecular Dynamics (MD), Dissipative Particle Dynamics (DPD) simulations, and the Gel Lattice Spring Model (GLSM) — to explore current challenging problems in the field.

Current challenges

  • Computational design of smart materials.
  • Phase separation kinetics in multi-component systems — simple fluids, polymer melts and solutions, and block copolymer melts and solutions.
  • Nanostructure formation in nonequilibrium systems.
  • Computational analysis of self-assembly involving atom transfer radical polymerisation (ATRP), free radical polymerisation (FRP), and photo-controlled radical polymerisation (Photo-CRP).

Research topics

Photo-responsive phase separation

How light can be used to trigger and steer phase separation in polymer melts — switching bonds or illumination patterns on and off to control domain growth and final morphology, rather than relying on temperature or composition alone.

Saini, Singh & Singh — Physical Review E, 112, 025407 (2025)
A. K. Singh, Chauhan, Puri & Singh — Soft Matter, 17(7), 1802–1813 (2021)

Miktoarm star polymers

Star-shaped polymers with arms of unequal length or chemistry self-assemble very differently from linear chains. We study how arm number, size, and rigidity set the pace and pattern of segregation and self-assembly.

Verma, A. K. Singh & Singh — Langmuir, 41(47), 31860–31874 (2025)
Gogoi, Puri & Singh — Journal of Chemical Physics, 164, 094905 (2026)
Gogoi, Chauhan, Puri & Singh — Physical Review E, 110(3), 034504 (2024)

Polymer-brush particles & Pickering emulsions

Anisotropic particles grafted with polymer brushes can sit at a fluid–fluid interface and stabilise an emulsion. We look at how particle shape, brush architecture, and grafting pattern govern how stable that emulsion is.

Pradhan, Upadhyay, Shrivastava, Singh & Saha — Langmuir, 41(25), 16342–16355 (2025)
Shrivastava, Upadhyay, Pradhan, Saha & Singh — Langmuir, 40(27), 13920–13934 (2024)
Pradhan, Upadhyay, Shrivastava, Singh & Saha — Polymer, 324, 128222 (2025)

Segregation kinetics in complex fluids

The core statistical-physics question underneath most of our work: once a mixture becomes unstable, how fast do domains grow, and what shape do they take? We track this in simple fluids, charged systems, and bond-disordered lattices.

Verma & Singh — Physical Review E (Letter), 113(3), L033401 (2026)
Shrivastava & Singh — Phase Transitions, 96, 311–327 (2023)
Gogoi, Chauhan, Puri & Singh — Soft Matter, 19(34), 6433–6445 (2023)
