Research

The research interests of our group lie broadly in the fields of Soft Matter and Statistical Physics, with a particular focus on nonequilibrium dynamics, phase separation, self-assembly, and the formation and evolution of complex structures.

We investigate how simple microscopic interactions give rise to rich structural and dynamical behaviour in soft materials. Our work spans phenomena such as nucleation, spinodal decomposition, phase separation, domain growth, coarsening, self-assembly, gelation, and interfacial dynamics. We are particularly interested in systems that evolve far from equilibrium, where morphology and dynamics can be strongly influenced by molecular architecture, confinement, interactions, external stimuli, and hydrodynamic effects.

We combine analytical theory with computational approaches to study these problems across multiple length and time scales. Our toolkit includes coarse-grained field-theoretical models, Monte Carlo (MC), Molecular Dynamics (MD), Dissipative Particle Dynamics (DPD), and the Gel Lattice Spring Model (GLSM). These approaches allow us to connect microscopic interactions and molecular architecture with experimentally relevant mesoscale structures and their evolution.

Current challenges

  • Understanding and controlling nonequilibrium structure formation and dynamics in complex soft materials.
  • Phase separation kinetics in multicomponent systems, including simple fluids, polymer melts and solutions, and block copolymer systems.
  • Understanding how molecular architecture, topology, interactions, and external stimuli control self-assembly and nanostructure formation.
  • Understanding the role of confinement, surfaces, interfaces, and curvature in controlling morphology and dynamics.
  • Computational design of smart and responsive soft materials.
  • Computational analysis of self-assembly and polymerisation processes involving atom transfer radical polymerisation (ATRP), free radical polymerisation (FRP), and photo-controlled radical polymerisation (Photo-CRP).

Research topics

Photo-responsive phase separation

Can phase separation be switched on and off using light? We study polymeric and amphiphilic systems whose interactions can be modified by photo-responsive bonds. By alternating between light-on and light-off states, we investigate how competing mixing and demixing processes generate evolving and multiscale morphologies.

We quantify these dynamics using correlation functions, structure factors, interfacial properties, fractal dimensions, and characteristic domain length scales.

Saini, Singh & Singh — Physical Review E, 112, 025407 (2025)
A. K. Singh, Chauhan, Puri & Singh — Soft Matter, 17(7), 1802–1813 (2021)

Miktoarm star polymers

Polymer architecture can dramatically change the way materials organize themselves. We study miktoarm star polymers in which chemically distinct polymeric arms are connected to a common core.

By varying parameters such as arm number, chain length, architecture, and molecular interactions, we investigate how polymer structure controls segregation kinetics, morphology, and self-assembly pathways.

Polymer-brush particles & Pickering emulsions

Polymer-functionalised particles can organise at fluid–fluid interfaces and strongly influence the behaviour of emulsions. We investigate how particle geometry, polymer-brush architecture, grafting density, and surface patterning control interfacial organisation and emulsion stability.

These systems provide a route to understanding how molecular design can be translated into controllable mesoscale structures and functional interfaces.

Gels, interfaces & confined soft materials

Soft materials often behave very differently when their motion is constrained by a network, surface, or interface. We use coarse-grained models such as the Gel Lattice Spring Model (GLSM), together with particle-based simulations, to explore how elastic networks, fluid interfaces, and confinement modify structural evolution and relaxation.

We are interested in how mechanical constraints and interfacial interactions influence deformation, rearrangement, pattern formation, and the dynamics of complex soft materials. These systems connect the physics of gels, polymers, colloids, and interfaces across multiple length scales.

Polymer topology at interfaces

Polymer topology can play a crucial role in determining how molecules organise at interfaces. We investigate how different polymer architectures and topologies modify interfacial structure, segregation, and dynamics, and how these effects differ from those observed in the bulk.

By studying polymers with different connectivity, architecture, and interactions at interfaces, we aim to understand how molecular topology translates into changes in mesoscale morphology and interfacial behaviour.
